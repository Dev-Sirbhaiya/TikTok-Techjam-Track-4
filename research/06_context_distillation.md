# Context Distillation, Session Memory, and Personalization for a Single-Session Shopping Copilot

Research scope: context distillation / memory compression in LLM-agent literature; short-term session-state representations (structured + soft); "long-term user profile" feasibility under a single-session eval regime; cheap online preference-updating; personalization's role in ranking vs retrieval; and session-based recommendation prior art. Conducted via web/arXiv search on 2026-08-26 for TikTok TechJam 2026 Track 4 ("Shopping Copilot").

---

## Overview

The task statement's Pillar III requirement — "leveraging accumulated dialogue history to continuously update short-term session state AND long-term user profiles" — sits at the intersection of three normally-separate literatures that have only recently started to merge under the banner of "context engineering" for LLM agents:

1. **LLM-agent memory/context management** — how to keep an agent's working context small, current, and useful across many turns without re-feeding a raw transcript every call (context compression, memory notes, working vs. episodic vs. parametric memory).
2. **Dialogue state tracking (DST)** — the classical task-oriented-dialogue mechanism for maintaining explicit structured slots (e.g., `category=sneakers`, `budget<80`, `color=black`) turn over turn. This is covered in depth elsewhere in this research set; here it is only referenced as the "explicit" half of session state.
3. **Session-based / sequential recommendation (SBR)** — a mature, LLM-independent RecSys subfield (GRU4Rec, SASRec/BERT4Rec, PEN4Rec, session-aware re-ranking) that already solves "build and continuously update a preference representation from a short interaction sequence and use it to improve the next-item ranking," which is structurally almost identical to what Track 4 needs.

The critical scoping issue is **term collision**: "context distillation" is an established, specific term in the LLM literature (Snell et al., 2022) meaning something different from what this track's framing implies. This is flagged explicitly in the "Key approaches" section and matters for correctly interpreting the requirement rather than chasing the wrong prior art.

## Key approaches found

### 1. "Context distillation" — two distinct meanings in the literature

- **Meaning A (the actual ML term of art):** Snell, Klein & Zhong, *"Learning by Distilling Context"* (arXiv:[2209.15189](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.15189), 2022) coined "context distillation" as a self-distillation objective: a model is fine-tuned (token-level KL) to reproduce the outputs it produces *when given* a prompt/scratchpad, but without the prompt/scratchpad present — i.e., baking a static context into the *weights* so the context tokens are no longer needed at inference. This is a **training-time** technique and is explicitly out of scope here (no FM training is allowed, and per-session weight updates make no sense for a single ~10-turn interaction). A 2026 follow-up, *"Context Distillation as Latent Memory Management"* (arXiv:[2605.28889](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.28889)), reframes the same idea as a memory-management move rather than a training move, which is closer to relevant, but still assumes a distillation/fine-tuning step per update.
- **Meaning B (what the track statement almost certainly intends — "context compression"/"memory distillation" in the applied agent-engineering sense):** Continuously compressing accumulated raw interaction history (turns, clicked/rejected items, stated constraints) into a compact, structured or vector representation that is cheap to re-inject into every retrieval/ranking call, without retraining anything. This is the dominant 2025–2026 usage in agent-engineering writing:
  - Anthropic, *"Effective context engineering for AI agents"* (Sept 2025) — frames context as a finite, curated resource; recommends structured note-taking / scratchpad patterns where an agent writes a compact external summary and re-reads it, rather than re-processing full history each turn. ([anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents))
  - *ACON: Optimizing Context Compression for Long-horizon LLM Agents* (arXiv:[2510.00615](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.00615)) and *ACE: Pluggable Adaptive Context Elasticizer* (arXiv:[2606.31564](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.31564)) — threshold-triggered compression: invoke a (cheap) summarizer once context exceeds a budget, rather than compressing every turn.
  - Mem0's engineering write-ups distinguish **context compression** (shrinking what's already in-context) from **memory** (durable, addressable facts extracted and stored outside the context window, retrieved on demand) — a "write-before-compaction" pattern extracts durable facts/preferences every turn *before* any compaction happens, so preferences survive even if raw turns are dropped. ([mem0.ai/blog/context-compression-vs-memory-in-ai-agents](https://mem0.ai/blog/context-compression-vs-memory-in-ai-agents))
  - *"Memory in the Age of AI Agents"* survey (arXiv:[2512.13564](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.13564), Dec 2025) argues the classic long/short-term dichotomy is insufficient and proposes a taxonomy along **forms** (token-level / parametric / latent memory), **functions** (factual / experiential / working memory), and **dynamics** (formation, evolution, retrieval). Useful vocabulary: our "short-term session state" is *working memory*, and a within-session preference vector is a form of *latent memory*. Companion paper list: [github.com/Shichun-Liu/Agent-Memory-Paper-List](https://github.com/Shichun-Liu/Agent-Memory-Paper-List).
  - OpenAI Cookbook, *"Context Engineering for Personalization — State Management with Long-Term Memory Notes"* — a directly applicable pattern: distill durable, natural-language "memory notes" from a conversation as it happens (not at the end), tagged separately from ephemeral session context, then retrieved and merged into the next turn's prompt.

  **Takeaway:** for Track 4, "distillation" should be read as Meaning B (compression/summarization into a compact reusable state), not Meaning A (weight internalization). Cite Meaning A only to explicitly rule it out as a red herring.

### 2. Short-term session-state: structured slots + soft/implicit signal

- **Structured slots** (DST interface, not re-derived here): a dict-like state (`category`, `price_band`, `brand`, `color`, `occasion`, `excluded_items`, `must_not_contain`) updated turn-by-turn from parsed intents, each optionally carrying a confidence/recency weight. Retrieval-Augmented Conversational Recommendation work explicitly formalizes this as "semi-structured natural language state tracking" fed into a retriever prompt (arXiv:[2406.00033](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.00033)) — i.e., the slot state doesn't have to be pure JSON; a short natural-language state string works as the retrieval-conditioning signal too.
- **Soft/implicit signal — an evolving preference vector:**
  - *Real-time Personalization using Embeddings for Search Ranking at Airbnb* (Grbovic & Cheng, KDD 2018) is the canonical production reference: maintains **short-term** click-set and skip-set centroid embeddings within a session, updated as new interactions occur, and combines them at ranking time with a static long-term (aggregated historical) user-type embedding. It uses **exponential recency weighting** to compress a variable-length interaction history into a fixed-size vector, on the stated assumption that the most recent actions are the most predictive.
  - *Session Context Embedding for Intent Understanding in Product Search* (arXiv:[2406.01702](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.01702)) — vectorizes the sequence of within-session queries + engagements (clicks/add-to-cart/orders) into a single session embedding usable for re-ranking and even query rewriting/retrieval.
  - *PEN4Rec* (arXiv:[2106.09306](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.09306), code: [github.com/zerohd4869/PEN4Rec](https://github.com/zerohd4869/PEN4Rec)) models preference *evolution* explicitly with a two-stage attention: stage one aggregates behaviors relevant to the most recent item, stage two models the trajectory of preference change over the session, specifically to resist "preference drift" disturbance — i.e., not just an average of past turns but an attention-weighted trend.
  - *Time-weighted Attentional Session-Aware Recommender System* (arXiv:[1909.05414](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1909.05414)) combines attention over past session items with an explicit time-decay weight per item — a good template for "attention over past turns weighted by recency."

  **Interface for our system:** two artifacts in session state — (a) a small structured slot dict (explicit, DST-style, cheap and inspectable, good for hard filters like price/category), and (b) a single dense preference vector (soft, continuous, good for semantic re-ranking) updated turn-by-turn by an exponentially-recency-weighted or attention-weighted combination of that turn's query/item embeddings. These are complementary, not competing — slots gate/filter candidates, the vector re-ranks within the filtered set.

### 3. Long-term user profile — reasoning under the single-session eval constraint

See dedicated section below; this is the most consequential ambiguity in the whole requirement.

### 4. Runtime-cheap online updates (no retraining)

- **EMA / exponentially-recency-weighted averaging** over item or query embeddings — used directly at Airbnb (above) and is the standard cheap online update: `v_t = α·e_t + (1-α)·v_{t-1}`, O(d) per turn, no gradient step, no persisted model.
- **Slot confidence + decay** — DST literature on **credibility decay**: newer slot-fills override older ones, and a slot's confidence can be decayed if not reaffirmed for several turns (mirrors "slot decay" already in the project's stated scope). Confidence-threshold gating is also used to decide whether to auto-apply vs. re-confirm a slot (see DST confidence-based update strategies, e.g. surveyed in TACL DST work: [direct.mit.edu/tacl DST with weak supervision](https://direct.mit.edu/tacl/article/doi/10.1162/tacl_a_00513/113662)).
- **Importance weighting by signal type** — explicit statements ("I want black, under $50") should dominate implicit clicks; clicks should dominate mere views/impressions. A simple weighted-sum update (not a learned model) suffices: assign a fixed weight per event type (explicit constraint > click/add-to-cart-like positive signal > dwell/view > impression) when folding a turn's evidence into both the slot state and the preference vector.
- **Negative signal handling** — Airbnb's dual click-embedding / skip-embedding (booked-listing-type vs. skipped-listing-type centroids) is a simple, effective way to represent "shown but rejected" as a distinct negative-preference vector subtracted (or contrasted) at ranking time, rather than only tracking positives.
- None of this requires training or persisted external state beyond the (in-memory, per-session) vector(s) and slot dict, which fits the "in-memory only" constraint well.

### 5. Personalization signals feeding RANKING (not just retrieval)

- Retrieval narrows the catalog to a candidate set (hard filters from slots + coarse semantic match). **Ranking** is where the soft preference vector should do the heaviest lifting: score candidates by a blend of (a) query/turn relevance and (b) similarity to the running preference vector, e.g. `score = λ·relevance(query, item) + (1−λ)·sim(pref_vector, item_embedding)`, optionally with the negative/skip vector subtracted.
- Prior art specifically for *re-ranking* (as distinct from generating candidates):
  - Pei et al., **PRM** — *Personalized Re-ranking for Recommendation* (RecSys 2019, arXiv:[1904.06813](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.06813)) — list-wise transformer re-ranker that models mutual influence between candidates in a list and injects a personalized embedding per user into the encoding, directly optimizing the final list rather than scoring items independently. This is the classic reference for "personalization changes ranking, not just candidate generation."
  - Airbnb (above) and the SIGIR eCom workshop paper *Real-Time Personalized Ranking in E-commerce Search* ([sigir-ecom.github.io/ecom21Papers/paper9.pdf](https://sigir-ecom.github.io/ecom21Papers/paper9.pdf)) both frame in-session embeddings as **ranking features/boosts layered onto a generic base ranker**, not as a separate retrieval stage — i.e., personalization is additive re-scoring on top of whatever the base relevance ranker already found.
  - Etsy's *Unified Embedding Based Personalized Retrieval* (arXiv:[2306.04833](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.04833)) and Yandex's *Personalized Transformer-based Ranking* (arXiv:[2310.03481](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.03481)) show the same pattern at larger scale: personalization embeddings blended into both retrieval and a downstream re-ranker.
  - **Practical implication for MTTC/MRR/Hit@K**: since our ranker only needs to reorder an already-small candidate set each turn, a lightweight additive re-scoring pass (no separate model) directly targets these metrics — later turns should show *improved* MRR/Hit@K on the same or narrowed candidate set as the preference vector sharpens, which is presumably exactly what "progressively better-targeted" is meant to reward.

### 6. Session-based recommendation as directly-transferable prior art

- **GRU4Rec** (Hidasi et al., 2016) and **SASRec** (Kang & McAuley, 2018, self-attentive sequential recommendation) are the foundational session/sequence models: given only the current session's item sequence (no persistent user ID needed), predict the next relevant item. This is architecturally the closest existing research problem to "single session, no cross-session history, must get better within the session" — exactly our eval setting.
- Surveys: *A Survey on Session-based Recommender Systems* (Wang et al., ACM CSUR) and *Sequence-Aware Recommender Systems* (arXiv:[1802.08452](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1802.08452)) catalog the family (item-KNN baselines → RNN → GNN → attention-based) and consistently find that **simple recency-weighted or attention-weighted aggregation baselines are strong and cheap**, with heavier sequence models providing diminishing marginal accuracy at much higher engineering cost — relevant given our hard runtime/complexity constraints.
- **PEN4Rec** and **TRUST** (arXiv:[2606.27214](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.27214)) specifically target *preference drift within a session*, i.e., the user's intent visibly changing mid-session (e.g., "actually, show me something warmer") — this is the realistic form of "preference evolution" a 10-turn shopping session will exhibit, as opposed to slow multi-session drift.
- Adapting these to a *conversational* (LLM-mediated) setting mainly means replacing "item ID sequence" with "turn-level embedding of (query text + engaged items + explicit constraints)" as the sequence unit, and replacing the trained sequence model with a cheap non-parametric aggregator (EMA/attention-weighted mean) since no training is allowed here.

## Interpretation of "long-term user profile" given the single-session eval constraint

This is flagged as required. Reasoning through the options:

**The core tension:** the track description explicitly requires updating "long-term user profiles," but the evaluation setup described to us is single-session-per-user with no multi-user concurrency and (implicitly) no persistent cross-session state store ("in-memory only," "no external... state store clusters," each session an isolated interaction). If each evaluation session is truly a one-shot isolated interaction with a fresh user, there is, by construction, no second session in which a genuine cross-session "long-term profile" could ever be *read back* and demonstrate value. That makes a literal, production-style long-term profile (persisted across sessions, reloaded at the start of a new session) **structurally unobservable and hence unrewarded by the stated metrics** (Hit Rate@K, MRR, MTTC are all computed within a session's turns).

Three candidate readings, evaluated:

- **(a) "Long-term profile" is just loose terminology for what is actually a within-session profile.** Under this reading, the graders are using "long-term" colloquially to mean "persists across the *whole conversation*, not just the current turn" — as contrasted with "short-term" meaning "just this turn's immediate slot state." I.e., "short-term state" = this-turn/this-exchange working memory, and "long-term profile" = accumulated-since-turn-1 session memory. This reading is **linguistically supported**: in several of the papers surveyed above (e.g., the LLM-based temporal-context paper and the OpenAI cookbook's own terminology), "short-term" vs. "long-term" is explicitly used to mean *recent few turns* vs. *the full session/conversation so far* — not necessarily *cross-session*. Given the hackathon's own hard constraint of "max 10 turns per session" and "each session is a single isolated user interaction," this is the most **operationally usable** interpretation: build one profile object per session that accumulates monotonically (weighted, decayed) over all turns seen so far, and treat "short-term" as its fast-moving recent-turn component and "long-term" as its slower-moving whole-session-history component (e.g., two EMAs with different decay rates, or a session-lifetime slot-confidence store vs. a last-N-turn attention window). This costs nothing extra to build and directly serves Hit@K/MRR/MTTC improvement across a session.

- **(b) "Long-term profile" refers to simulated preference drift/persona the system must adapt to within the session**, i.e., the evaluation harness might seed a synthetic persona (a labeled preference profile) for the simulated user and test whether the agent's "profile" converges toward matching it as turns progress, or the "profile" must survive being challenged (e.g., a distractor turn) without forgetting earlier-established preferences. This is plausible if the benchmark/session dataset includes such structure (a persona label used only for scoring adherence, not given to the agent). We could not confirm this from the problem statement alone; it is consistent with "self-evolution" framing (the emphasis on getting "progressively better" implies there's a moving target being converged upon, which could be a hidden persona rather than pure recency-fit). If true, this would still be implementable with the same single-session mechanism as (a) — nothing changes technically, only the interpretation of *why* it should improve metrics.

- **(c) A genuine cross-session, multi-user persistent profile store**, literally read/written across independent sessions (e.g., a returning "user_id" reused in a labeled eval set spanning several short sessions each). This is the interpretation that matches the literal words "long-term" and "profiles" most closely, and it is explicitly what most of the cited LLM-personalization papers (OpenAI's Context Engineering cookbook, "Enabling Personalized Long-term Interactions," arXiv:[2510.07925](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.07925)) are actually built for. But it directly **contradicts** the given constraints: "in-memory only (no external vector DB/state store clusters)" strongly implies no durable storage layer is expected to survive process/session boundaries, and "each session is a single isolated user interaction — no multi-user concurrency stress needed" reads as an explicit statement that the eval harness does not exercise cross-session continuity. Building this would add real engineering risk (persistence layer, user-identity resolution, staleness handling) for a capability the grading setup likely cannot observe or reward.

**Best interpretation and recommendation:** treat (a) as primary — implement "long-term user profile" as a **within-session, slower-decaying accumulation layer** distinct from a **fast-moving short-term/recent-turn layer**, both scoped to the single session. This satisfies the literal requirement ("continuously update... long-term user profiles" — it is continuously updated, and it is longer-lived than the immediate turn state) while staying inside every stated hard constraint (in-memory, single isolated session, no concurrency). As a low-cost hedge against reading (b) or a hidden persona-adherence metric, design the profile object to be easily *inspectable/loggable* (e.g., a short natural-language or structured summary emitted each turn) so that if the eval harness does check profile-vs-hidden-persona convergence, the signal is legible; and keep the persistence layer abstracted behind a small interface (e.g., a `ProfileStore.get(session_id)/save(session_id, profile)`) so that if it turns out sessions *are* grouped by a reused user id across a small labeled set, upgrading from (a) to a real (c)-style store is a small, low-risk change rather than a redesign — but do not build (c)'s complexity (external DB, expiry policies, concurrency-safe writes) speculatively, since the given constraints argue against needing it and it burns implementation time against a 10-turn, no-training, no-UI hackathon scope.

**Flag to the team:** this ambiguity should ideally be resolved by re-reading the exact track rubric/eval-harness wording (if more of it exists beyond what was quoted to us) for any hint that sessions are grouped by a persistent user id, or that a hidden persona/preference-drift label is used in scoring. Absent that, proceed with (a) plus the (c)-shaped interface hook described above.

## Relevance to our constraints

- **10-turn cap:** favors cheap, incremental, streaming updates (EMA, attention over a short window) over anything requiring a full-history re-encode per turn — full-history summarization every turn would be wasteful when the whole session is at most 10 turns anyway; a running vector update is strictly cheaper and just as expressive at this scale.
- **Catalog read-only, no FM training:** rules out any approach that fine-tunes embeddings or a ranker on the fly (rules out Meaning-A "context distillation," rules out learned session models like GRU4Rec/SASRec trained per-session). All recommended techniques below are **non-parametric, weight-free** updates (weighted averaging, attention with fixed/precomputed weights, decay schedules) — consistent with what several of the strongest SBR baselines already show is sufficient.
- **In-memory only, no external state-store clusters:** favors keeping session state as a small in-process object (slot dict + one or two fixed-size vectors), which is trivially "in-memory" and imposes no infra.
- **Single isolated session, no multi-user concurrency:** directly informs the long-term-profile interpretation above; also means we don't need any locking/consistency mechanism around profile updates.
- **Text-only, no UI:** the profile/session-state object can be a plain structured object (dict/dataclass) or short natural-language string re-injected into the LLM prompt each turn — no need for embedding visualizations or dashboards.
- **Evaluated on Hit Rate@K, MRR, MTTC:** all three reward *getting to the right item faster*, which is best served by the ranking-time boost (Section 5) rather than by retrieval-time filtering alone — a preference vector that sharpens turn-over-turn should measurably lift MRR/Hit@K in later turns and reduce MTTC, which is a good internal validation check to build regardless of which long-term-profile interpretation is chosen.

## Recommended approach(es) with rationale

1. **Two-tier session state, both in-memory, both scoped to the session (per Interpretation (a) above):**
   - *Short-term:* explicit slot dict (DST-style, high-confidence hard constraints: price band, category, explicit exclusions) refreshed every turn, with confidence decay for slots not reaffirmed recently (ties to the project's existing "slot decay" scope) — cheap, inspectable, used at **retrieval/filtering** time.
   - *Long-term (session-scoped):* a slower-decaying preference vector (or two: positive-affinity and negative/rejected-affinity, à la Airbnb's click/skip embeddings), updated turn-by-turn via **exponential recency weighting or lightweight attention over past turns**, importance-weighted by signal type (explicit statement > click/like > view > impression). Used at **ranking/re-ranking** time as an additive score boost over base relevance, following the PRM/Airbnb/Yandex pattern (Section 5).
2. **Update rule:** `pref_vec ← normalize(α · embed(this_turn_signal) + (1−α) · pref_vec)`, with α tunable (higher α = short-term-weighted, lower α = long-term-weighted); optionally run two instances at different α to get an actual short-term/long-term pair without needing two separate architectures.
3. **Ranking-time use:** `final_score(item) = λ·base_relevance(query, item) + (1−λ)·cos_sim(pref_vec, item_embedding) − μ·cos_sim(neg_vec, item_embedding)`, with λ (and optionally μ) possibly increasing turn-over-turn as confidence in the accumulated profile grows (mirrors how PEN4Rec and time-weighted attentional models let recent/converged signal dominate).
4. **Distill for the LLM prompt, not just for ranking:** in addition to the numeric vector, maintain a short natural-language "memory note" string (few dozen tokens) summarizing accumulated preferences/constraints, refreshed incrementally (write-before-compaction pattern) — this is what actually gets fed back into the LLM's context each turn cheaply, while the raw transcript can be safely truncated/dropped. This directly operationalizes "context distillation" in the applied sense (Meaning B) without ever needing Meaning A's fine-tuning step.
5. **Keep a `ProfileStore` interface seam** (even if backed by a plain dict keyed by session id in v1) so that if it turns out the eval harness does group sessions by a reused user id, extending to genuine cross-session persistence is a config change, not a redesign — but do not build the persistence backend now.

## Dos

- Do treat "long-term profile" as the slower-moving, whole-session-history component and "short-term state" as the fast-moving recent-turn component, both computed and held purely in-memory for the duration of the single session.
- Do use simple, weight-free online updates (EMA, decayed attention, confidence decay) — they are cheap, match the 10-turn budget, and are well-supported as strong baselines in the session-based recommendation literature.
- Do let personalization act primarily as a **ranking-time additive boost** on top of a base relevance/retrieval score, not only as a retrieval filter — this is what the Hit@K/MRR/MTTC metrics actually reward turn-over-turn.
- Do track negative signal (skipped/rejected items) separately from positive signal, not just positives.
- Do keep a compact natural-language "memory note" distilled incrementally each turn (write-before-compaction) as the thing re-fed into the LLM prompt, rather than re-summarizing the full transcript every call.
- Do make the profile/session-state object inspectable/loggable, as a hedge in case the eval harness scores persona-adherence or drift-tracking rather than pure recency-fit.

## Don'ts

- Don't conflate this task with Snell et al.'s "context distillation" (fine-tuning a model to internalize a prompt) — that requires training and is explicitly disallowed and irrelevant at this scale.
- Don't build a persistent, externally-stored, cross-session/cross-user profile database — it contradicts the "in-memory only" and "single isolated session" constraints and cannot be exercised or rewarded by a single-session eval.
- Don't re-encode/re-summarize the entire raw transcript from scratch every turn when an incremental update (EMA/attention-window/append-only memory notes) achieves the same effect far more cheaply within a 10-turn budget.
- Don't adopt a trained sequential-recommendation model (GRU4Rec/SASRec/BERT4Rec proper, with learned weights) — the "no FM training" and no-retraining constraints rule these out; use them only as a conceptual template for the update rule, not as literal architecture.
- Don't let the profile only gate retrieval (candidate filtering) and stop there — without a ranking-time effect too, the required "progressively better-targeted" behavior won't show up in MRR/Hit@K on the already-narrowed candidate set.
- Don't ignore negative/rejected signal — treating only positive engagement as preference evidence throws away information that's cheap to capture (shown-but-not-selected items) and that the Airbnb-style dual-embedding pattern shows meaningfully improves ranking.
